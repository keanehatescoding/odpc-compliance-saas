# Kinga: ODPC compliance for Kenyan SMEs

Kinga ("protection" in Swahili; a placeholder name) helps small organisations meet their obligations under the Data Protection Act, 2019:

- **Registration tracker.** Record your ODPC data controller and/or processor certificates. Each one gets a status (not started, application pending, active, renewal due, expiring soon, expired) calculated in Nairobi time, plus the indicative renewal fee for your organisation size.
- **Renewal reminders.** A daily job emails reminders 90, 60, 30, 14, 7 and 1 days before expiry, on the day itself, and 7, 14 and 30 days after. Each reminder is sent once per certificate cycle. Reminders stop once you record a renewal application.
- **Breach response.** Log a personal data breach the moment you learn of it. A live countdown tracks the s.43 deadline: 72 hours to notify the ODPC as a controller, or 48 hours to notify the controller as a processor. The clock stops when you record the notification, or when a controller records why harm is unlikely. You record the risk assessment, affected RoPA activities, containment steps and a dated incident log, then generate draft notifications for the ODPC and for affected people. The team gets an email when a breach is logged, with 24 hours left, and when it becomes overdue.
- **RoPA builder.** A record of processing activities: purpose, s.30 lawful basis, data subjects, categories, sensitive data, cross-border transfers, retention and security. It includes sector templates (schools, clinics, SACCOs, fintech, retail, hospitality), DPIA screening, CSV export and a printable view.
- **Privacy notice.** Section 29 says people must be told how their data is used before it's collected, so Kinga writes a notice from the RoPA: who the organisation is and how to reach it (contact details from Settings), what each activity collects and why, its legal basis, whether giving the data is optional (the activity's own answer; only consent has standard wording, because a contract or a law doesn't settle it for everything an activity collects), who receives it, transfers outside Kenya, how long it's kept, how it's protected, people's rights with the response times, and how to complain to the ODPC. It cites the controller certificate while it's current, and adds a children section where an activity involves them. Pick a category of data subject, such as parents or staff, to get a notice covering just their data. It prints, or downloads as an HTML page for a website. It covers the activities the organisation controls; each RoPA activity records whether it's the controller or a processor, and what it processes for someone else is left out. So is an activity typed in before that question existed, until someone answers it. An audience the RoPA doesn't have is a 404, not the notice for everyone. Things to add or confirm before publishing are listed above the notice, naming the activities concerned: no contact details, an unconfirmed role, no security measures, no answer on whether the data is required, a transfer without safeguards, no recipients for an activity that has processors, a consent-based activity with no consent record or no recorded way to withdraw, or the wording about a parent's consent.
- **Impact assessments.** Full s.31 DPIAs: describe the processing, justify necessity and proportionality, then score each risk before and after mitigation on a likelihood × severity matrix. Start from a flagged RoPA activity, which pre-fills the facts and the matching sector template, or from a template for new processing (CCTV, student and patient records, KYC, credit scoring, marketing, biometric attendance). A DPIA can't be approved while sections are empty or a risk remains high without a recorded ODPC consultation. Approved DPIAs are due for review after 12 months, and each one has a printable report.
- **Data subject requests.** Log requests from people exercising their rights: access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection, portability and opting out of third-party marketing. Each type gets its deadline from the Data Protection (General) Regulations, counted from the day the request arrived: 7 days for access and marketing opt-outs, 14 for correction, erasure, restriction and objection, and 30 for portability. Record who asked, anyone acting for them, how you confirmed their identity, and your response. Declining requires written reasons, and the request page shows what each type allows. The team gets an email 2 days before the deadline and again once it passes, and open requests appear in the dashboard's next steps.
- **Consent records.** Section 32 puts the burden of proving consent on the controller, so each thing people are asked to agree to gets a record: the exact wording, how it's given (signed form, online tick-box, SMS opt-in, spoken), where and when it's asked, where the proof is kept, how someone withdraws, and a review date. Each belongs to a RoPA activity. It's a record of how consent is obtained, not a store of individual consents: the signed forms and opt-in logs stay where the record says they are. A record is marked proof incomplete (no wording, no proof location or no way to withdraw), in place or review due, and its page lists what it still needs: a parent's or guardian's consent where the activity involves children (s.33) and how that person is verified, a linked activity whose lawful basis isn't consent, or a service refused to people who don't agree (s.32(4)), with a checklist of what makes consent valid. The list shows activities that rely on consent with no record, and an activity's RoPA page lists its consent records or prompts for one. Missing and incomplete records and reviews that have come due appear in the dashboard's next steps.
- **Processors.** A register of the suppliers that handle personal data for the organisation, such as a payroll bureau, a school or clinic system, a bulk SMS service or a cloud host. Section 42 says a controller must choose processors that give sufficient guarantees about security and have a written contract with each, so every entry records what they do, which RoPA activities they handle, where they hold the data, how their security was checked, and when the contract was signed and is next due for review. Each one is marked no written contract, contract in place or review due, and its page lists what the record still needs, including a processor outside Kenya whose activities don't record a transfer, with a checklist of what the contract should say. An activity's RoPA page lists its processors. Missing contracts and reviews that have come due appear in the dashboard's next steps.
- **Staff training.** A log of the data protection training staff have had. Each session records when it was held, who ran it, who it was for, how many came, what it covered, where the attendance register is kept and when the refresher is due. Kinga counts attendees and doesn't name them. A session is marked up to date, refresher due or refresher held. Recording the refresher as a new session, from the button on the earlier one's page, links the two, so the earlier one stops being due; deleting the refresher makes it due again. A refresher has to be held after the session it refreshes. Each session's page lists what the record still needs, with a checklist of what training should cover. Having no training on record, and refreshers that have come due, appear in the dashboard's next steps.
- **Compliance report.** One printable page saying where the organisation stands, for a board, an auditor or an ODPC inspector. It goes area by area (registration, the RoPA, the privacy notice, DPIAs, breaches, consent, data subject requests, processors and staff training), giving the figures, the records behind them and what is still open: an expired certificate, a role the RoPA shows but no registration covers, an activity screening flags with no DPIA, an activity relying on consent with no consent record, a breach not yet notified, a request past its deadline, a processor without a contract, a training refresher that has come due. Breaches, requests and training sessions are counted over the last 12 months, with how many breaches and requests were notified or answered in time, and anything older stays on while something on it is outstanding. Requests are counted by type and never listed by requester, so the report names no data subject. It says what has been recorded, not that the organisation complies.
- **Team.** Owners and admins invite colleagues by email from the Team page. The link works for 7 days, only for the invited address, and once. Opening it either creates an account (already confirmed, since the link proves the address) or, for an existing account, asks them to sign in and join. Each account belongs to one organisation. Roles: members work on all the records; admins also change settings and manage admins and members; owners also manage other owners and get the compliance emails. An organisation always keeps at least one owner. Removing someone takes away their access straight away. Invitations can be resent or withdrawn, and each person can send 20 an hour.
- **Activity history.** Each change to registrations, the RoPA, DPIAs, breaches, data subject requests, processors, staff training, consent records, the team, settings and automatic renewal is logged with who made it and when, in the same transaction as the change. Edits name the fields that changed, and approving a DPIA or recording a response to a request gets its own entry. The Activity page lists everything, filtered by area, and each record's page shows its own history. Entries keep the person's name after their account is deleted, and outlive the records they describe, but a request is named by its type and date rather than the requester, so erasing it leaves no name behind. The log is included in the organisation's export and deleted with the organisation.
- **Billing.** New organisations get a 14-day free trial. After that they pay through Paystack by M-Pesa or card: KSh 3,000 a month for micro and small organisations, KSh 5,000 for medium and KSh 8,000 for large (the size set in Settings), or ten times that for a year. Payments are prepaid. Each one adds a month or a year after the trial or current period ends, so paying early loses nothing. A payment is credited once, by whichever arrives first: Paystack's signed webhook or the payer's return from checkout, which looks the transaction up with Paystack. Owners get an email 3 days before access ends and another once it has, and the app shows a banner in the last 7 days. Once access lapses, registrations, the RoPA, DPIAs, data subject requests, processors, staff training and consent records become read-only until someone pays. Breaches, the team and settings keep working, so a lapsed organisation can still meet the 72-hour deadline, and exports, printouts, renewal reminders and deadline alerts carry on. Each payment gets a numbered receipt, printable from the Billing page and emailed to whoever paid. It shows the organisation's name and KRA PIN as they were when it was paid. Owners and admins can pay; members see the status.
- **Automatic renewal.** Someone paying by card can tick "Save my card and renew automatically". If Paystack says the card can be charged again, it's saved and charged for the same plan as each period ends: a day before, then, if that fails, 1 and 3 days after. Owners are told the amount and card 3 days ahead instead of being asked to pay, get a receipt for each charge, and an email for each failure. After the third failure renewal turns off (the card stays saved). A card that will have expired by the end date isn't charged, and the reminder says so. Each charge is claimed in the database before it's made, with a reference fixed by the organisation, period and attempt, so overlapping jobs can't charge twice, and a charge whose outcome was lost is looked up before trying again. From the Billing page, owners and admins can switch between monthly and annual, turn renewal off, or remove the card (which also deactivates it on Paystack). Owners are emailed when renewal is turned off or the card removed. M-Pesa payments can't be charged again, so they don't renew.
- **Expert services.** Owners and admins can order an expert DPIA review or a compliance audit from the Services page, at a fixed price for the organisation's size, saying what they want covered. Payment goes through the same Paystack checkout and gets a numbered receipt, but doesn't change the subscription, so a lapsed organisation can still order. Once paid, the order is emailed to `SELLER_EMAIL`. Staff move it through in progress, delivered or cancelled from the staff pages or with `npm run service-order`, and owners get an email when it's delivered. Everyone in the organisation sees its orders and their status.
- **KRA eTIMS invoices.** Once eTIMS is set up, each credited payment is sent to KRA as a sale through its OSCU API and signed. The receipt then shows the control unit invoice number, internal data, receipt signature and date, and a QR code that opens KRA's check for the invoice; until KRA signs it, the receipt says the invoice is pending. Kinga isn't VAT-registered, so every line is tax type D (Non-VAT) and prices are unchanged. The buyer's KRA PIN goes on the invoice when it's a valid one. An invoice that doesn't go through is retried by the hourly job, with the same invoice number, for 10 attempts. Refunds are made in the Paystack dashboard. When Paystack reports one processed, Kinga records it and sends KRA a credit note for the amount refunded, citing the original invoice. The credit note waits until KRA has signed that invoice, and is retried the same way. The receipt lists each refund with its credit note.
- **Account security.** New accounts confirm their email before using the app, because renewal reminders and breach alerts go there. The link is single use, expires in 24 hours, and never signs anyone in. Until it's opened, the user can resend it (5 an hour) or fix a mistyped address, and owners who haven't confirmed get no compliance emails. Password reset by emailed link (single use, expires in 1 hour, signs you out on every device). Signed-in users can change their password in Settings by confirming their current one (10 tries per 15 minutes). This signs out their other devices, cancels any reset links and emails them a notice. Login, signup and reset attempts are rate-limited per IP address, and sign-in attempts per email address (cleared when you sign in), with counters kept in Postgres so every app instance shares them.
- **Staff pages.** People whose confirmed address is in `STAFF_EMAILS` can open `/staff`. Organisations lists every organisation with its trial, paid or lapsed state, owners, team size, how many records of each kind it keeps and when someone last changed one, filtered by state, with totals at the top. Each organisation's page adds its team, saved card, payments, automatic renewal charges and service orders. Service orders is the queue of paid orders not yet delivered, with what the customer asked for, and buttons to start, deliver or cancel each one. Staff never see what the records say, as the DPA promises; signed-in people not on the list get a 404.
- **Kinga's own compliance.** Public Terms of Service (`/terms`), Privacy Notice (`/privacy`) and Data Processing Agreement (`/dpa`) pages, linked from every footer. Kinga is the controller of account and billing data, and the processor of the records organisations keep in it, so the DPA covers s.42's processor terms: 48-hour breach notice, sub-processors (listed in `src/lib/legal.ts`) and deletion at the end. Signing up, directly or by invitation, means ticking that you agree, and each account records the version and when (`TERMS_VERSION`). From Settings, owners and admins can download everything the organisation keeps as one JSON file, leaving out secrets such as password hashes and the card's Paystack authorization. Owners can delete the organisation by typing its name and their password. Its records, team, invitations and saved card go at once (the card is also deactivated on Paystack) and everyone in it gets an email. Paid payments stay with their refunds and eTIMS invoices for the 5 years tax law requires, under an organisation row marked deleted. Deletion waits while a paid service order is still open. Anyone can delete their own account the same way, unless they're the organisation's only owner. Before a change to the Terms, DPA, Privacy Notice, sub-processors or prices, `npm run legal-notice` emails every owner what's changing and when, at least 30 days ahead as the Terms and DPA promise. Each owner gets it once, and the hourly job sends it to anyone who becomes an owner before the date.

## Stack

Next.js 16 (App Router, server actions), TypeScript, Tailwind 4, Postgres with Drizzle ORM, zod, and vitest (with PGlite for database tests).

## Getting started

```bash
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d        # or point DATABASE_URL at any Postgres 14+
npm install
npm run db:migrate
npm run db:seed             # optional demo data
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000. The seed creates a school, "Sunrise Academy", whose controller certificate expires in 20 days and whose processor certificate expired 17 days ago, an open breach with 42 hours left to notify the ODPC, an approved DPIA for student records, CCTV flagged as needing a DPIA, a parent's access request due in 2 days, two processors (one with a contract due for review next month, one outside Kenya with no contract), two training sessions (one whose refresher fell due a month ago), a consent-based activity for pupil photographs with two consent records (one complete, one that can't be proved yet), a second team member, a pending invitation and a paid annual subscription. Sign in as `demo@kinga.test` / `demo-password-1` (an owner) or `otieno@kinga.test` with the same password (a member).

## Scheduling reminders and alerts

Run the job every hour, because breach deadlines are counted in hours. Renewal reminders still go out at most once per threshold. Run it either as a script:

```bash
npm run reminders
```

or over HTTP (for Vercel Cron, GitHub Actions and similar):

```bash
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET" https://your-host/api/cron/reminders
```

It runs seven jobs: renewal reminders, breach alerts, alerts for data subject requests that are close to or past their response deadline, charging saved cards for automatic renewal, billing emails for trials and subscriptions that are about to end or have ended, resending eTIMS invoices and credit notes KRA hasn't signed yet, and emailing owners about changes to the terms that haven't taken effect yet. The endpoint returns `{ reminders, breaches, requests, renewals, billing, etims, notices }` with the results of each job (`renewals` and `etims` are null when Paystack or eTIMS isn't set up). All seven are safe to run more than once. Each (certificate, expiry date, threshold) is claimed in `reminder_log`, each (breach, alert stage) in `breach_alert_log`, each (request, alert stage) in `subject_request_alert_log`, and each (organisation, email, end date) in `billing_alert_log`, before sending. The claim is released if the send fails. Each (notice, owner) is claimed the same way in `legal_notice_log`, but marked sent only once the email has gone, and a claim left unmarked for 30 minutes, because the run died mid-send, is taken over by the next run, so an owner may hear of a change twice but always hears of it. Each renewal charge is claimed in `renewal_attempts` (organisation, end date, attempt) before the card is charged, and is never released, so a card is charged at most once per attempt. After downtime, each job sends only the most recent missed alert, not every one. While a renewal charge is with Paystack, the job holds a lock on that organisation and one database connection, for at most Paystack's 15-second timeout. Organisations are charged one after another, so a run never holds more than one, but each overlapping run holds its own, and the pool has 10 (`src/db/index.ts`). Use one scheduler, and don't start the job by hand while it's running; the GitHub Actions workflow queues its runs for this reason. Meanwhile that organisation's billing changes, deletion and incoming payments wait. If Paystack is slow, watch for pool saturation. Logging a breach or a request also triggers its first alert straight away, and editing a request so that its deadline moves clears its alerts so they are sent again for the new deadline.

## Deploying

Run the app behind a reverse proxy. Rate limiting reads the client IP from a header that the proxy sets, so tell the app which header to trust:

- **Default (`TRUST_IP_HEADER=x-forwarded-for`, `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS=1`):** the app uses the rightmost `X-Forwarded-For` entry. This fits one proxy that appends the client address, such as Vercel, Caddy's `reverse_proxy`, or nginx with `proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-For $proxy_add_x_forwarded_for`.
- **More than one proxy** (for example a CDN in front of nginx): set `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` to the number of proxies that append an entry. With the default of 1, every client would share the CDN's address and one bucket.
- **`TRUST_IP_HEADER=x-real-ip`:** use this only if your proxy overwrites `X-Real-IP` on every request (nginx: `proxy_set_header X-Real-IP $remote_addr`). Caddy and nginx pass a client-supplied `X-Real-IP` through unchanged unless configured to overwrite it, so a client could pick a new address on each request.

If `next start` is exposed directly, clients can send their own `X-Forwarded-For` and dodge the per-IP limits. The per-email login limit still applies.

`APP_URL` must be the public URL, because password reset links and the Paystack return URL are built from it.

In production, missing `APP_URL` or `SMTP_URL` makes every request fail with a 500, so the deploy fails its health check instead of going live and silently not sending breach alerts or reset links.

Server errors are logged as one JSON line with `"event":"server_error"`, and the error page shows the user that line's `digest` as a reference. Set up a log alert on `server_error` in your host (Railway: Observability → Log alerts), or add an error tracker such as Sentry in `src/instrumentation.ts`.

`next.config.ts` sets a Content Security Policy (no third-party scripts, styles, frames or form targets apart from Paystack checkout), HSTS and other security headers. Allow any new third-party origin there before using it.

### Railway

`railway.json` builds the app with Railpack, runs `npm run db:migrate` before each deploy and starts it with `npm start`. Create a project with a Postgres database and a service from this repository, then set the service's variables: `DATABASE_URL=${{Postgres.DATABASE_URL}}`, `APP_URL` (its public domain), `CRON_SECRET` and the others above.

`.github/workflows/reminders.yml` runs the hourly job by calling `/api/cron/reminders`. In the repository's Actions settings, add a variable `APP_URL` and a secret `CRON_SECRET` with the same value as the service's. GitHub can start scheduled runs a few minutes late, which the job allows for.

### Payments

1. Set `PAYSTACK_SECRET_KEY`. Use the test key (`sk_test_…`) until you've tried a payment end to end.
2. In the Paystack dashboard (Settings → API Keys & Webhooks), set the webhook URL to `https://your-host/api/paystack/webhook`. Payments are credited without it, when the payer comes back from checkout, but anyone who closes the tab first would wait for support. The webhook also tells Kinga about refunds, so their eTIMS credit notes go out.
3. Enable M-Pesa (mobile money) as a payment channel for your Paystack Kenya account. Checkout offers whatever channels the dashboard has turned on.
4. Run the reminder job at least hourly (see above): it charges saved cards, so a renewal can only be as punctual as the job.
5. Set the `SELLER_*` variables to the legal name, KRA PIN, address and email of the business taking payments, so receipts carry them.

### eTIMS

KRA signs each sale through its OSCU API, so getting there takes KRA's involvement:

1. On the [eTIMS portal](https://etims.kra.go.ke), apply for OSCU as the business taking payments, and ask for sandbox access. KRA gives you a device serial number for the sandbox.
2. Set `ETIMS_URL=https://etims-api-sbx.kra.go.ke/etims-api`, `ETIMS_TIN` (the business's KRA PIN), `ETIMS_BHF_ID` (`00` for the head office) and `ETIMS_DEVICE_SERIAL`, then run `npm run etims:init`. It prints `ETIMS_CMC_KEY` and `ETIMS_SDC_ID`; set them. Keep the key secret. KRA only hands it out once per device.
3. Find a classification for software and compliance services with `npm run etims:items -- --classes service`, set `ETIMS_ITEM_CLASS` to its code, and run `npm run etims:items` to register the monthly plan, the annual plan and each service (codes `KE3NTU0000001` onwards; never reuse a number for something else).
4. Make test payments and check the receipts show a signed invoice and that the QR code opens KRA's page for it. KRA vets the integration before giving production credentials.
5. Repeat steps 2 and 3 with `ETIMS_URL=https://etims-api.kra.go.ke/etims-api` and the production serial.

Only payments credited while eTIMS is set up get an invoice. Invoice numbers come from `etims_invoice_seq`, starting at 1; if the branch has issued invoices through another system, move it past them first (`SELECT setval('etims_invoice_seq', <last number>)`). An invoice the hourly job gives up on stays `failed` in `etims_invoices` with the reason in `last_error`, and is logged by `npm run reminders`. If KRA answers that it already has the invoice number, an earlier attempt went through but its answer was lost: look the signature up on the eTIMS portal.

A refund gets a credit note only if its payment has an invoice. Credit notes take the next number from the same sequence, are numbered `CN-` plus that number on our side, and use reason code 06 (Refund). A credit note isn't sent until its invoice is `signed`, so if an invoice has `failed`, fill in its signature from the portal and set it to `signed`, and the hourly job then sends the credit note. Refunding doesn't change a subscription's paid period.

The migration that adds receipts numbers any payments already credited, in the order they were paid, and bills them to each organisation's current name and KRA PIN.

The migration that adds billing gives every existing organisation a 14-day trial from the moment it runs, so current users aren't locked out on deploy.

## Caveats (verify before launch)

This is an MVP, not legal advice. Check these against current ODPC guidance:

- **Fees** in `src/lib/dpa.ts` are indicative, and the org-size bands have no thresholds attached.
- **24-month certificate validity** is assumed as the default expiry. Users can override it per certificate.
- **KRA PIN format** is validated as `A/P + 9 digits + letter`.
- The **penalty wording** in reminder emails (up to KSh 5M or 1% of turnover).
- **DPIA screening** is a simple heuristic, not the ODPC's official criteria.
- **DPIAs**: the risk matrix and its high/medium/low bands are a heuristic. Treating "high risk after mitigation" as the trigger for s.31 prior consultation, the 12-month review default and the template contents are our reading. Check them against the ODPC's DPIA guidance, including any rule requiring the DPIA to be submitted to the ODPC before processing starts.
- **Data subject request deadlines** in `src/lib/subject-request.ts` are calendar days from receipt, taken from regs. 7–12 and 18 of the Data Protection (General) Regulations, 2021. A deadline falling on a Sunday or public holiday isn't moved to the next working day. The 7-day limit for telling someone you've declined a correction or portability request is shown as guidance rather than tracked, because the Regulations don't say when it starts.
- **Breach rules**: the 72-hour and 48-hour deadlines, the "real risk of harm" test, the exemption from telling affected people when the data was unintelligible, and the particulars in the draft notification are summarised from s.43. Check them, and the ODPC's current submission channel, against the Act and the ODPC's guidance.
- **Staff training.** The Act doesn't require training in so many words. `src/lib/training.ts` reads it into s.41's organisational measures and the capacity-building duty s.24(7) gives a data protection officer, and the report cites s.41. The 12-month refresher is a suggestion in the form, not a rule, and the list of what training should cover is ours. Check all three against the ODPC's guidance.
- **Consent records.** The checklist of what makes consent valid, and what `src/lib/consent.ts` counts as a gap, are our reading of ss.2, 32 and 33: that a record without the wording, a proof location and a withdrawal route can't prove consent; that a service refused without consent may not be freely given; and that any activity marked as involving children needs a parent's or guardian's consent. Section 33 has exceptions (a child seeking counselling or child-protection services) the app doesn't model, and the Act doesn't set a review interval. Check them against the ODPC's guidance on consent. Kinga doesn't hold individual consents or withdrawals.
- **The compliance report** in `src/lib/compliance-report.ts` cites a section of the Act for each area and decides what counts as open (a certificate inside its 90-day renewal window with no application, for instance). Both are our reading. A breach is open from the moment it's logged until it has been assessed, notified and, where needed, the people affected told, and closing it in the app doesn't change that. A request is open only once its deadline has passed. Training is open when none is recorded, when a refresher date has passed with no refresher recorded, or when the last session is more than 12 months old. The 12-month period is a choice, not a rule.

- **Prices** in `src/lib/plans.ts` follow the brief's KSh 3,000–8,000 a month range. Organisations choose their own size in Settings, as they do for ODPC fees, so check it when you invoice larger customers.
- **Automatic renewal** depends on the card's bank allowing charges without the payer present. Some banks ask for approval (Paystack reports the charge as `paused`), which counts as a failure here, so try it with a live card from your customers' main banks before relying on it. Paystack has no way to charge M-Pesa again.
- **Service prices** in `src/lib/services.ts` are placeholders. Set them, and the turnaround times, before launch.
- **eTIMS** follows KRA's OSCU API specification v2.0. The sale fields, the tax type D treatment for a seller not registered for VAT, the payment method codes and the QR code's link (`…/common/link/etims/receipt/indexEtimsReceiptData?Data=<PIN><branch><signature>`) need checking against the sandbox and KRA's vetting. Register for VAT once turnover passes KSh 5M a year, and then the tax type and prices must change. Credit notes are sent as `rcptTyCd` R with the refunded amount as a positive figure and `salesSttsCd` 02, since the spec says nothing about signs or the credit note's own status; check both in the sandbox.
- **Kinga's legal pages** in `src/app/(legal)/` are a starting draft, not reviewed by a lawyer. Have them reviewed, in particular the liability cap, the refund terms and the transfer safeguards. Set `SELLER_NAME`, `SELLER_ADDRESS` and `SELLER_EMAIL` so the pages name the real business and a contact. Update `SUBPROCESSORS` in `src/lib/legal.ts` with the email provider's name and each provider's actual locations, and keep it in step with the deployment. For a change that matters, send `npm run legal-notice` at least 30 days ahead, as the Terms promise. The pages change as soon as they're deployed, so deploy the new text, and bump `TERMS_VERSION`, on the date the notice gives. Until then the summary in the email is all owners see of the change, so make it say everything that matters. Kinga needs its own ODPC registration as a controller and processor. The Privacy Notice says deleted data lingers in backups until overwritten, so make sure your backups do expire.
- **Payments that arrive after deletion.** A checkout started before an organisation was deleted and paid afterwards isn't credited. The webhook logs `arrived after its organisation was deleted`, and you refund it in the Paystack dashboard.
